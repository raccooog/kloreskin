How to Actually Figure Out Your Skin Type

Most people guess wrong, especially when what looks like dry skin is actually dehydrated skin — two completely different problems with completely different fixes.

K Kloré Skin • Updated July 30, 2026 • 6 min read

Quick answer: Wash your face, apply nothing, and check back after 30 minutes. Shiny all over means oily. Shiny only in the T-zone means combination. Tight and flaky means dry. Comfortable with no shine or tightness means normal. If skin feels tight but doesn't flake, it's often dehydrated rather than truly dry — a hydration problem, not an oil-production problem.

The Bare-Face Test

  1. Wash your face with a gentle cleanser.
  2. Pat dry and apply nothing else — no toner, no moisturizer.
  3. Wait 30 minutes and check how skin looks and feels.
  4. Wait another 30 minutes (an hour total) for a clearer read, especially if the first check was ambiguous.

This works because it removes every product variable and shows what skin actually does on its own, rather than how a moisturizer or serum is currently making it behave.

The Four Main Skin Types

Type What You'll See
Oily Shine across the entire face, including cheeks, within the hour
Dry Tightness, possible flaking, no shine anywhere
Combination Shiny T-zone (forehead, nose, chin), normal or dry cheeks
Normal Comfortable, balanced, no significant shine or tightness

Dry vs. Dehydrated — The Mistake Almost Everyone Makes

Dry skin lacks oil (a skin type, generally consistent over time). Dehydrated skin lacks water (a temporary condition that can affect any skin type, including oily skin). This distinction gets missed constantly, and it changes what actually helps.

Dry Skin Dehydrated Skin
Cause Low natural oil production Low water content, can happen to any skin type
Fixed by Richer, more emollient products Humectants like hyaluronic acid, sealed with moisturizer
Can oily skin have this? Rarely Yes, oily and dehydrated at once is common

If you've tried rich, heavy products for "dry skin" without improvement, the actual issue may be dehydration — our hyaluronic acid guide covers the humectant approach that dry-skin products alone don't address.

What to Do With Each Type

Quick Reference

  • ✔ Oily: lightweight, gel-based products, niacinamide to help regulate oil
  • ✔ Dry: richer moisturizers, ceramides, gentle cleansers
  • ✔ Combination: lightweight on the T-zone, richer on cheeks, or a balanced formula overall
  • ✔ Normal: focus shifts to prevention and maintenance — sunscreen, consistent hydration

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming oily skin can't also be dehydrated, and skipping hydration entirely.
  • Treating dehydration with heavier oils instead of a humectant plus moisturizer combination.
  • Testing skin type right after a shower or hot beverage, which can temporarily skew results.
  • Assuming skin type never changes — it can shift with seasons, age, and hormones.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can my skin type change over time?

Yes, skin type can shift with age, seasons, hormones, and climate, so it's worth reassessing periodically rather than assuming it's fixed forever.

Can I have oily and dry skin at the same time?

That combination usually describes combination skin (oily T-zone, drier cheeks) or oily-but-dehydrated skin, rather than true dry and oily coexisting on the same area.

Does my skin type determine my whole routine?

It's a strong starting point for product texture and richness, but specific concerns like acne, redness, or dark spots still need their own targeted ingredients regardless of skin type.

How often should I retest my skin type?

Retesting each season, or after any major routine or life change, helps catch shifts before they cause ongoing irritation or breakouts.

Final Thoughts

Knowing your actual skin type — and specifically whether "dryness" is really dehydration — changes almost every product decision after it. It's the one test worth doing before buying anything new.
