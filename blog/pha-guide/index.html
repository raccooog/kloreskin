PHA: The Exfoliant for Skin That Can't Handle AHA or BHA

If glycolic acid leaves you red and salicylic acid stings, PHA does a similar smoothing job with molecules too large to penetrate as deeply — and that's exactly the point.

K Kloré Skin • Updated August 6, 2026 • 5 min read

Quick answer: PHA (polyhydroxy acid, most commonly gluconolactone or lactobionic acid) exfoliates the skin's surface similarly to AHA, but its larger molecular size means it penetrates more slowly and gently, making it well suited for sensitive, rosacea-prone, or eczema-prone skin that reacts poorly to AHA or BHA. It also has a mild humectant, antioxidant effect that AHA and BHA don't offer.

How PHA Actually Works

PHA works through the same basic mechanism as AHA — loosening the bonds between dead skin cells on the surface — but its molecules are significantly larger, which slows how deeply and quickly it penetrates skin. This translates to a gentler experience with less irritation, while still delivering a genuine smoothing and brightening effect over consistent use. Common forms include gluconolactone and lactobionic acid.

PHA vs. AHA and BHA

PHA AHA BHA
Molecule size Larger, penetrates slowly Smaller, penetrates faster Oil-soluble, goes into pores
Irritation risk Lowest of the three Moderate Moderate
Best for Sensitive, reactive, or barrier-compromised skin Dullness, uneven texture Oily, acne-prone, clogged pores

Who It's Especially Good For

  • Anyone who has tried AHA or BHA and found them too irritating.
  • Rosacea-prone skin, following the pattern in our rosacea guide, where harsh exfoliants are generally discouraged.
  • Eczema-prone or barrier-compromised skin recovering from irritation.
  • Beginners to chemical exfoliation who want the gentlest possible entry point.

How to Use It

Quick Start

  • ✔ Can often be used more frequently than AHA/BHA, up to daily for some formulas, due to lower irritation risk
  • ✔ Still follow with moisturizer and sunscreen, as with any exfoliant
  • ✔ A reasonable starting point for anyone new to chemical exfoliation generally
  • ✔ Can be layered with other gentle actives like niacinamide or cica

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming "gentle" means "ineffective" — PHA still delivers a genuine exfoliating benefit over time.
  • Skipping sunscreen, since any exfoliant increases sun sensitivity somewhat.
  • Overlooking PHA as an option after a bad experience with AHA or BHA, rather than trying the gentler alternative.
  • Expecting the same speed of visible results as a stronger acid — PHA's gentleness means a slightly longer timeline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is PHA as effective as AHA?

It's gentler and generally slower-acting, but with consistent use it can deliver comparable smoothing benefits for many people, particularly at a maintenance level.

Can I use PHA every day?

Many PHA formulas are gentle enough for daily use, though it's worth starting a few times a week and monitoring tolerance.

Is PHA good for rosacea?

Yes, it's one of the more commonly recommended exfoliant options for rosacea-prone skin due to its lower irritation potential.

Can I combine PHA with retinol?

Many people do, since PHA's gentleness makes it one of the easier exfoliants to pair alongside other actives, though it's still worth introducing gradually.

Final Thoughts

PHA proves that exfoliation doesn't have to mean irritation. For skin that's struggled with AHA or BHA, it's often the missing option that finally makes chemical exfoliation sustainable rather than something to dread.
