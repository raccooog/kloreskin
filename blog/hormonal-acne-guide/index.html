Hormonal Acne: Why It Shows Up Where It Does

Breakouts along the jawline and chin that follow a monthly pattern aren't random bad luck — they follow a predictable hormonal cycle, and that changes what actually helps.

K Kloré Skin • Updated July 30, 2026 • 6 min read

Quick answer: Hormonal acne typically shows up along the jawline, chin, and lower cheeks, often as deeper, tender bumps rather than surface whiteheads, and tends to flare on a roughly monthly cycle. It's driven by hormonal fluctuations that increase oil production and inflammation. Skincare can meaningfully reduce severity — especially BHA and niacinamide — but severe or persistent hormonal acne often benefits from a conversation with a dermatologist about options skincare alone can't address.

Why It Shows Up on the Jawline and Chin

The lower face has a higher concentration of androgen-sensitive oil glands, meaning it responds more strongly to the hormonal fluctuations that increase oil production and inflammation. This is why hormonal breakouts cluster in this area rather than appearing evenly across the whole face.

These breakouts also tend to sit deeper in the skin — often felt before they're visible — because hormonal fluctuations trigger inflammation deeper in the pore, not just surface-level clogging.

Recognizing the Cyclical Pattern

Sign Hormonal Pattern
Location Jawline, chin, lower cheeks
Timing Recurs around the same point in a monthly cycle
Texture Deeper, tender bumps rather than surface whiteheads
Response to typical acne products Often more resistant than surface-level breakouts

Keeping a simple log of when and where breakouts appear over a couple of months makes this pattern much easier to confirm than trying to recall it after the fact.

What Skincare Can Actually Help With

A Reasonable Routine

  • ✔ BHA (salicylic acid), 2–3 times a week, to keep pores clearer ahead of a flare
  • ✔ Niacinamide daily, to help regulate the oil surge that drives breakouts
  • ✔ A calming step like cica during the actual flare, rather than adding new actives
  • ✔ Consistent, gentle cleansing rather than aggressive washing during flares

Our AHA vs BHA guide and niacinamide guide cover these two ingredients in more depth — both are reasonable first steps before considering anything more intensive.

When to Talk to a Dermatologist

Skincare alone often reduces severity but doesn't fully resolve hormonal acne, since the underlying driver is hormonal rather than purely topical. If breakouts are painful, cystic, leaving scarring, or not responding to a consistent routine over a few months, that's a reasonable point to discuss options like hormonal treatments or prescription-strength topicals with a dermatologist.

Common Mistakes

  • Over-exfoliating or over-cleansing during a flare, which tends to worsen inflammation.
  • Constantly switching products mid-cycle instead of giving one routine consistent time to work.
  • Picking at deeper, tender bumps, which increases the risk of scarring and PIH.
  • Assuming skincare alone should fully resolve a hormonally-driven pattern.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can diet affect hormonal acne?

Some people notice a connection with high-glycemic or dairy-heavy diets, though the relationship varies significantly by individual.

Is hormonal acne only related to menstrual cycles?

It's most commonly discussed in that context, but hormonal fluctuations from other causes can produce a similar jawline pattern.

Will hormonal acne go away on its own?

It can fluctuate with life stages and hormonal changes, but it often benefits from active management rather than waiting it out.

Should I stop all actives during a hormonal flare?

Not necessarily, but simplifying to gentler, more calming steps during the flare itself, and resuming stronger actives once it settles, is a reasonable approach.

Final Thoughts

Hormonal acne responds to a pattern most people don't realize is a pattern until they start tracking it. Skincare can meaningfully reduce severity, but for anything persistent or severe, pairing it with a conversation about the underlying hormonal driver tends to get further than skincare alone.
