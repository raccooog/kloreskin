Sleeping Packs: Are They Just a Thicker Moisturizer?

Korean sleeping packs get marketed as an overnight transformation step, sitting one shelf over from regular night moisturizer. The honest answer to whether they're different is: mostly, yes, but not dramatically.

K Kloré Skin • Updated August 11, 2026 • 5 min read

Quick answer: Sleeping packs are typically richer, more occlusive, and applied as a thicker final layer than a regular moisturizer, designed to seal in hydration overnight when skin loses more moisture. They work through the same principle as heavier moisturizing generally — there's no unique "overnight" mechanism beyond providing a stronger occlusive barrier during the hours skin naturally loses the most water.

What Sleeping Packs Actually Are

A sleeping pack (or sleeping mask) is typically a richer, more occlusive final step applied as the last part of a night routine, designed to sit on skin undisturbed for several hours of sleep. The format leans into the fact that skin loses more water overnight, as covered in our sleep and skin guide — a thicker occlusive layer helps counter that moisture loss more effectively than a lighter daytime moisturizer would.

Sleeping Pack vs. Regular Moisturizer

Sleeping Pack Regular Moisturizer
Texture Typically richer, gel-cream or balm-like Varies, often lighter for daily wear
When used Final night step, left undisturbed Morning and/or night, worn under makeup or clothing
Occlusive strength Generally higher Varies by product

In practice, a sufficiently rich regular moisturizer, applied generously as the final night step, can achieve a very similar effect to a dedicated sleeping pack — the format isn't magic, it's mainly about richness and occlusion.

Who Benefits Most From One

  • Dry or dehydrated skin types, especially in winter or dry climates.
  • Anyone who already found benefit from occasional slugging, since a sleeping pack functions similarly but with a more cosmetically pleasant texture.
  • People who want an occasional, more intensive step without committing to a full slugging routine every night.

How to Use One Correctly

Quick Start

  • ✔ Apply as the final step of your night routine, after all serums and regular moisturizer
  • ✔ Use a slightly thicker layer than a standard moisturizer, since it's designed to sit undisturbed
  • ✔ A few times a week is reasonable for most skin types, rather than necessarily every single night
  • ✔ Very oily or acne-prone skin may prefer using it only occasionally, if at all

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming a sleeping pack contains some unique "overnight" technology beyond richer occlusion.
  • Using it nightly on oily or acne-prone skin, where the heavier texture may contribute to congestion.
  • Buying both a sleeping pack and a rich regular moisturizer, when one well-chosen richer product often covers both roles.
  • Skipping the rest of the routine (serums, actives) and relying on the sleeping pack alone for the whole night's benefit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need both a moisturizer and a sleeping pack?

Not necessarily — many people simply use a rich moisturizer as their final step rather than layering a separate dedicated sleeping pack.

Can I use a sleeping pack every night?

For dry skin, often yes. For oily or acne-prone skin, a few times a week may be more comfortable than nightly use.

Is a sleeping pack the same as slugging?

They achieve a similar occlusive effect, though sleeping packs are generally formulated to feel more cosmetically pleasant than a pure occlusive like petroleum jelly.

Should I wash off a sleeping pack in the morning?

Yes, cleansing in the morning removes any residue before starting the day's routine.

Final Thoughts

Sleeping packs aren't a separate category of magic — they're a richer, more occlusive final step that leans into skin's overnight moisture loss. A sufficiently rich moisturizer applied generously can do much the same job, making the dedicated product more about preference and texture than a unique mechanism.
