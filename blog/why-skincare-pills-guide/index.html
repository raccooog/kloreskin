Why Your Skincare Pills (And How to Actually Stop It)

Those little grey balls that show up when you rub in sunscreen or makeup aren't a sign a product "doesn't work" — they're a specific, fixable layering problem.

K Kloré Skin • Updated August 11, 2026 • 5 min read

Quick answer: Pilling happens when incompatible product textures or ingredients (often silicone-based and water-based formulas layered together, or too many products applied too quickly) fail to blend and instead roll up into visible balls. The fix is usually giving each layer more time to absorb, applying fewer products, or adjusting the order — not switching every product involved.

What Actually Causes Pilling

Pilling happens when a layer of product hasn't fully absorbed or set before the next one is applied, causing the two to mix on the surface and roll up into small balls or flakes when rubbed. It's more of a mechanical, textural issue than a sign either product is defective — it's about timing and compatibility, not quality.

The Most Common Trigger

Silicone-based products (many primers and some sunscreens) and certain water-based gels or serums don't always blend smoothly when layered too quickly, since silicones sit on the surface rather than absorbing the way water-based formulas do. This connects to our silicones guide — the silicone itself isn't the problem, but its surface-sitting nature makes timing more important when layering.

How to Fix It

Practical Fixes

  • ✔ Wait 60–90 seconds between each layer, letting the previous product fully absorb
  • ✔ Apply thin layers rather than thick amounts of any single product
  • ✔ Pat products in rather than rubbing, especially for the final layer before makeup
  • ✔ Reduce the total number of layers if pilling persists, rather than troubleshooting each one

This connects directly to the thin-to-thick logic covered in our serum layering guide — proper order and timing prevent most pilling before it starts.

When It's the Product, Not the Technique

If pilling happens consistently with the same two specific products regardless of timing and technique, the formulas themselves may simply be incompatible — some ingredient combinations (particularly certain silicone and water-based gel pairings) don't blend well no matter how carefully they're applied. In that case, swapping one of the two products, rather than continuing to troubleshoot technique, is the more practical fix.

Common Mistakes

  • Applying every step of a routine back-to-back with no time for absorption in between.
  • Rubbing vigorously to try to "fix" pilling once it's already started, which usually makes it worse.
  • Assuming pilling means a product isn't absorbing or isn't good quality, when it's usually a layering timing issue.
  • Adding more product on top of pills instead of gently removing them and reapplying with more time between steps.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does pilling mean my skincare isn't working?

No, it's a surface-level layering issue, not a sign the product itself is ineffective or not absorbing beneath the surface.

Why does my sunscreen pill under makeup specifically?

Sunscreen and makeup often both contain silicones or similar film-forming ingredients, and applying makeup before sunscreen has fully set is a common cause.

Can I remove pilled product and start over?

Yes, gently wiping away the pills and reapplying with more time between layers, or a lighter amount of product, generally resolves it.

Is pilling more common with certain skin types?

It can happen to any skin type, though very dry or flaky skin can sometimes make pilling more visible since the flakes catch and roll along with the product.

Final Thoughts

Pilling is almost always a timing or layering issue, not a reflection on any single product's quality. Slowing down between steps and applying thinner layers resolves it in most cases without needing to replace anything.
