Winter Skincare: What to Actually Change in a Dry Climate

The exact routine that felt perfect all summer can leave skin tight and flaky the moment indoor heating turns on. Here's what actually needs to change, and what doesn't.

K Kloré Skin • Updated July 29, 2026 • 6 min read

Quick answer: Cold air holds less moisture, and indoor heating dries it further, which pulls water out of skin faster than in warmer months. The fix isn't a whole new routine — it's usually a richer moisturizer, an added occlusive layer, and easing off strong actives slightly, while keeping the core steps (cleanser, hydrating serum, sunscreen) the same.

Why Skin Behaves Differently in Winter

Cold air naturally holds less moisture than warm air, and indoor heating systems dry that air out further, sometimes down to levels drier than a desert. Combined, this pulls moisture out of skin through the barrier faster than skin can replace it — which is why the same products that felt sufficient in summer can suddenly leave skin tight, flaky, or dull.

This isn't a sign the routine stopped working — it's a sign the environment around it changed, and the routine needs a proportional adjustment, not a complete overhaul.

What to Actually Change

Step Winter Adjustment
Cleanser Switch to a cream or gentle low-foam cleanser if using a stripping gel cleanser
Moisturizer Move to a richer, more occlusive formula, ideally with ceramides
Hydrating serum Keep hyaluronic acid, but always seal it immediately with moisturizer
Extra step Add an occasional occlusive layer (slugging) during especially dry weeks

What to Leave Alone

  • Sunscreen — UV exposure doesn't stop in winter, and snow reflects UV, increasing exposure in some conditions.
  • Niacinamide and cica, which are generally well tolerated year-round without adjustment.
  • Your overall routine order — the sequence of steps doesn't change, just the richness of individual products.

Adjusting Actives for the Season

Skin is generally more vulnerable to irritation when its barrier is already under stress from dry air, so this is a reasonable season to ease off frequency for retinol and AHA/BHA slightly, rather than pushing to increase strength or frequency.

Winter Active Adjustments

  • ✔ If using retinol nightly, consider dropping to every other night if skin feels tight
  • ✔ Reduce AHA/BHA frequency slightly if flaking increases
  • ✔ Add a recovery night with cica or ceramides between active nights
  • ✔ Increase, don't decrease, moisturizer richness during active nights

This pairs naturally with the rotation described in our skin cycling guide — the same framework, just with more recovery nights built in during winter months.

Common Mistakes

  • Keeping a summer-weight gel moisturizer through winter and wondering why skin feels tight.
  • Dropping sunscreen because it's cold or overcast.
  • Continuing full-strength actives at the same frequency despite visible dryness or flaking.
  • Over-layering heavy products without addressing the humidity issue at its source (a humidifier can help significantly indoors).

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I still need sunscreen in winter?

Yes, UV exposure continues year-round regardless of temperature, and snow can even increase UV exposure through reflection.

Should I stop using retinol in winter?

Not necessarily, but reducing frequency slightly if skin feels more sensitive than usual is a reasonable adjustment.

Does a humidifier actually help skin?

Yes, adding moisture back into dry indoor air can meaningfully reduce how much water skin loses overnight.

Why does my skin feel oily and dry at the same time in winter?

This can happen when the barrier is dehydrated and compensates by producing more oil — treating the underlying dryness, rather than just the oil, usually resolves both.

Final Thoughts

Winter doesn't call for a different routine, just a richer one — the same core steps, adjusted for an environment that's actively pulling more moisture out of skin than usual. Match the routine to the air around you, and skin follows.
