Dark Spots After a Breakout: Understanding PIH

The flat, discolored mark left behind after a pimple heals isn't a scar — it's usually something more temporary, and more treatable, than most people assume.

K Kloré Skin • Updated July 27, 2026 • 6 min read

Quick answer: Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) is a flat, discolored mark left behind after skin inflammation, like a healed pimple. Unlike textured acne scarring, PIH is not permanent — it typically fades on its own over 3–12 months, faster with consistent sunscreen use, gentle exfoliation, and brightening ingredients like niacinamide or vitamin C.

What PIH Actually Is

When skin experiences inflammation — from a breakout, a cut, or even an aggressive skincare reaction — it can respond by producing extra melanin in that spot as part of the healing process. That extra pigment is what shows up as a flat, brown, red, or purplish mark once the original blemish has healed.

It's a normal part of how skin heals, not a sign that anything went uniquely wrong, and it tends to be more common and longer-lasting in deeper skin tones due to more reactive melanin production.

PIH vs. Actual Scarring

PIH Textured Scarring
Texture Flat, same texture as surrounding skin Indented, raised, or uneven texture
Color Brown, red, or purple discoloration Can be discolored or match skin tone
Timeline Fades over months, often on its own Generally permanent without treatment
Best addressed by Brightening ingredients, sunscreen, time Professional treatments (peels, microneedling, lasers)

Run a finger over the mark with your eyes closed — if you can't feel any difference in texture, it's very likely PIH, not scarring.

How to Fade It Faster

What Actually Helps

  • ✔ Daily sunscreen — sun exposure darkens PIH and slows fading significantly
  • ✔ Niacinamide, used consistently over weeks to months
  • ✔ Vitamin C in the morning, layered under sunscreen
  • ✔ Gentle AHA exfoliation to support skin renewal, 2–3 times a week

Both niacinamide and vitamin C support fading PIH through different mechanisms, which is part of why they're often recommended together rather than as substitutes for each other.

Why Sunscreen Matters Most

UV exposure is the single biggest factor that keeps PIH visible longer than it needs to be. Sun exposure stimulates more pigment production in the exact spots already producing extra melanin, which can re-darken a mark that was otherwise fading. Skipping sunscreen while trying to treat PIH is, in practice, working against every other product in the routine.

Common Mistakes

  • Picking or popping blemishes, which increases inflammation and the likelihood of PIH forming in the first place.
  • Skipping sunscreen while actively trying to fade dark spots.
  • Over-exfoliating in an attempt to speed things up, which can cause more inflammation and more PIH.
  • Expecting results within days rather than the realistic months-long timeline.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does PIH take to fade?

Commonly 3–12 months, depending on skin tone, depth of the original inflammation, and consistency of sun protection and treatment.

Can PIH come back after it fades?

The original mark won't return, but new breakouts in the same area can create new PIH, which is why prevention matters as much as treatment.

Is PIH the same for every skin tone?

It can appear more pronounced and take longer to fade in deeper skin tones, due to more active melanin production during healing.

Do I need a dermatologist for PIH?

Not usually — most PIH responds well to consistent sunscreen and brightening ingredients at home. A dermatologist becomes more relevant for textured scarring or marks that aren't fading after many months.

Final Thoughts

PIH looks discouraging because it lingers, but it's one of the more predictable, treatable things skin does after a breakout. Consistency and sun protection do more here than any single product — patience is genuinely part of the treatment.
