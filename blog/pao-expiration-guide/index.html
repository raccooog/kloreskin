What That Little Jar Symbol on Your Skincare Actually Means

An open jar icon with a number and an "M" inside sits quietly on nearly every bottle you own — and it's telling you something most people never notice.

K Kloré Skin • Updated August 1, 2026 • 5 min read

Quick answer: The open jar symbol is the PAO (Period After Opening) icon, and the number inside (like "12M" or "6M") tells you how many months a product stays good for once it's opened — not from the manufacture date. This matters more than an expiration date on the box, since opening exposes the product to air and bacteria that speed up degradation.

What the Symbol Actually Means

PAO stands for Period After Opening. The number inside the jar icon (commonly 6M, 12M, or 24M) tells you how many months the product remains safe and effective once opened and exposed to air, not how long it lasts sealed on a shelf. A product can sit unopened for years and still be fine, but the countdown starts the moment it's first used.

This is different from a printed expiration date, which some products also have — when both are present, whichever comes first (the PAO countdown from opening, or the printed date) is the more conservative estimate to follow.

Typical Timelines by Product Type

Product Type Typical PAO
Vitamin C serum 3–6 months (oxidizes quickly)
Sunscreen 12 months, or per printed date
Moisturizers, cleansers 12–24 months
Sheet masks (single-use) Use immediately once opened

Vitamin C's short window connects directly to what's covered in our vitamin C guide — proper storage and timely use both matter for keeping it effective.

Signs a Product Has Gone Bad

  • A noticeable change in color, especially vitamin C turning yellow or brown.
  • Separation that doesn't blend back together when shaken.
  • An off or sour smell different from how it smelled when new.
  • A change in texture — clumping, thinning, or an unusual film on top.

Any of these signs are reason to stop using a product, even if it's technically still within its PAO window.

How to Make Products Last Their Full Window

Storage Tips

  • ✔ Keep products out of direct sunlight and away from heat
  • ✔ Close caps tightly and immediately after use
  • ✔ Use clean hands or a spatula rather than dipping fingers into jars
  • ✔ Store vitamin C and other sensitive actives in a cool, dark spot

Products in pump or airless bottles generally last closer to their full PAO window than jars, since they're exposed to less air and contamination with each use.

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming an unopened product's shelf life applies once it's been opened and used for months.
  • Continuing to use a product with a clear color or smell change, assuming it's still "probably fine."
  • Storing serums and actives in a warm bathroom instead of a cooler spot.
  • Not writing the opening date somewhere, making it hard to track the PAO countdown accurately.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it dangerous to use an expired product?

It can be, particularly for eye products and anything applied near broken skin, since degraded preservatives allow more bacterial growth over time.

Does sunscreen actually expire?

Yes, and using expired sunscreen can mean reduced UV protection even if the product still looks and smells normal.

What if there's no PAO symbol on my product?

A general rule of thumb is 12 months for most leave-on products and up to 24 months for simple, stable formulas, though checking for visible signs of degradation is still the more reliable method.

Can I still use a product past its PAO if it looks fine?

The PAO is a general guideline rather than an exact cutoff, but combined with any visible changes in color, smell, or texture, it's a reasonable point to stop using it.

Final Thoughts

That small jar icon is one of the more overlooked pieces of information already sitting on every bottle. Checking it takes a few seconds and can save both money and skin from a product that's quietly past its best.
