Can You Actually Shrink Your Pores?

"Minimizing pores" is on half the products in every skincare aisle. Here's what pore size actually depends on, and what that claim really means in practice.

K Kloré Skin • Updated July 28, 2026 • 5 min read

Quick answer: No product can permanently shrink pores — their size is determined mostly by genetics and doesn't change. What skincare can do is make pores look less noticeable: clearing out the oil and debris that stretch them, controlling excess oil production, and keeping surrounding skin smooth so pores don't stand out as much by contrast.

What Actually Determines Pore Size

Pore size is largely determined by genetics, sebum production, and age-related loss of elasticity around the pore opening. No topical product changes the physical structure or size of the pore itself — this is why "pore-shrinking" claims on packaging describe a visual, temporary effect rather than a permanent change.

Oilier skin types tend to have more visible pores, particularly in the T-zone, since higher sebum production is associated with larger pore openings on average.

Why Pores Look Bigger Some Days Than Others

Pores appear larger when they're stretched by trapped oil, dead skin, and debris, or when surrounding skin is dehydrated and the difference in texture becomes more visible. This is why the same pores can look far more noticeable after a long day than right after cleansing.

Sun damage over time also reduces skin elasticity around pores, which is part of why pores often look more pronounced with age, independent of oil production.

What Actually Helps Them Look Smaller

Approach Why It Works
BHA (salicylic acid) Clears oil and debris from inside the pore, reducing stretching
Niacinamide Helps regulate oil production over time
Daily sunscreen Preserves elasticity around pores, slowing age-related enlargement
Consistent hydration Keeps surrounding skin smooth, reducing visual contrast

Common Mistakes

  • Buying a product specifically because it says "pore-minimizing," expecting a permanent structural change.
  • Over-exfoliating in an attempt to shrink pores, which can irritate skin and make pores look worse.
  • Using pore strips frequently, which can stretch pores further with repeated use.
  • Ignoring sunscreen, which contributes to the long-term elasticity loss that makes pores more visible.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do pore strips actually shrink pores?

They can remove surface debris temporarily, but they don't change pore size, and frequent use may stretch pores further over time.

Does cold water close pores?

No, pores don't have muscles that open and close. Cold water can make skin look temporarily less oily, which creates the appearance of smaller pores.

Can retinol help with pore appearance?

Yes, by supporting skin turnover and elasticity, retinol can make pores look less pronounced over consistent use.

Is it normal for pores to look bigger with age?

Yes, reduced skin elasticity around pores is a common part of skin aging, independent of oil production.

Final Thoughts

Pore size itself isn't something skincare changes — but what fills them, and how smooth the skin around them looks, absolutely is. Aim for less visible, not smaller, and the goal becomes a lot more achievable.
