Slugging: The Overnight Trick for a Damaged Barrier

Sealing your whole routine under a layer of occlusive balm looks strange, but it's one of the simplest ways to help a dry or irritated barrier recover overnight.

K Kloré Skin • Updated July 26, 2026 • 5 min read

Quick answer: Slugging means applying an occlusive layer, usually petroleum jelly, as the final step in your night routine to seal in everything underneath and prevent water loss overnight. It's most useful for dry or barrier-damaged skin during recovery periods, and generally not recommended for oily or acne-prone skin, where it can trap oil and contribute to breakouts.

What Slugging Actually Is

Slugging is the last step of a night routine: after everything else has absorbed, a thin layer of an occlusive product — typically petroleum jelly — is applied on top. Occlusives don't add hydration themselves; they form a physical seal that dramatically slows water loss through the skin overnight.

It's essentially the most extreme version of what a heavy moisturizer already does, which is why it works well specifically when the barrier needs extra help holding onto moisture, rather than as an everyday routine step.

Who Should Try It

Situation Worth Trying?
Very dry, flaky, or barrier-damaged skin Yes, especially during recovery periods
After a night of retinol or acids Sometimes, if skin feels unusually tight or irritated
Oily or acne-prone skin Generally no — can trap oil and worsen breakouts
Normal, well-balanced skin Not necessary as a regular step

If your skin regularly feels tight and dry, it's worth checking whether a ceramide-based moisturizer alone solves the problem before reaching for slugging every night — occlusion is a recovery tool, not a permanent routine upgrade for most skin types.

How to Do It Properly

Quick Start

  • ✔ Complete your full routine first — cleanser, treatment, moisturizer
  • ✔ Apply a thin layer of an occlusive as the very last step
  • ✔ Use it a few nights a week during dry spells, not as a nightly habit
  • ✔ Change pillowcases more frequently while slugging regularly

A thin layer is enough — more doesn't seal any better and just increases the chance of feeling greasy or transferring product onto bedding.

Common Mistakes

  • Slugging every single night regardless of skin type or actual dryness level.
  • Using it directly over active ingredients like retinol without checking if that combination suits your skin.
  • Applying too thick a layer, which mostly just transfers onto pillows and clothing.
  • Trying it while actively breaking out, which can trap oil and worsen congestion.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will slugging clog my pores?

For most non-oily skin types, no — petroleum jelly itself is non-comedogenic, but it can trap excess oil underneath on already oily or congested skin.

Can I slug every night?

You can, but it's usually more useful as an occasional recovery step during dry periods rather than a permanent nightly habit.

Can I slug over retinol?

Some people do to reduce dryness, but it can also increase retinol's intensity by trapping it against skin longer — start cautiously if trying this combination.

Is petroleum jelly safe for sensitive skin?

Generally yes, it's considered one of the more inert, low-irritation occlusives available.

Final Thoughts

Slugging isn't a daily glow trick — it's a recovery tool for skin that's lost moisture faster than it can hold onto it. Used occasionally, on the right skin type, it can make a genuinely rough week look and feel a lot better by morning.
